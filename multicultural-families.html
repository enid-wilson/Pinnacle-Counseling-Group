Specialty Focus

Multicultural couples and families.

For intercultural partnerships and families building a third culture — the one that belongs to the relationship itself.

Cultural difference by itself is not the determining variable in intercultural marriage. What a couple does with that difference is.

For decades, the research question about intercultural marriage was essentially the wrong one: does cultural difference make a marriage harder? The honest answer, drawn from a wide body of research, is that findings conflict — some studies find intercultural couples report more difficulty; others find no difference; a smaller but notable body finds equal or greater satisfaction. The disagreement is itself informative. Cultural difference by itself is not the variable. What a couple does with that difference is.

Support With ✶

Areas of focus.

Intercultural Couples

Partnerships bridging cultural, ethnic, religious, or national backgrounds — and the work of building a third culture that belongs to the relationship itself rather than to either family of origin.

Bicultural Identity Integration

Active integration of both cultural identities into shared life — the research consistently associates this with higher relationship satisfaction, contrary to the assumption that one culture must recede for peace to hold.

Multicultural Families & Children

Family-systems work for households navigating multiple heritages. Children raised in identity-integrated families do not show significantly different identity outcomes than monocultural peers — but the family’s work matters.

Immigrant & Diaspora Experience

The particular clinical considerations of adjustment, generational tension, and identity across immigration, migration, and diasporic contexts.

Interfaith Partnerships

The specific work of couples navigating religious and spiritual difference alongside cultural difference — and the intentionality required to build practices that honor both.

Conflict Rooted in Cultural Frames

Disagreements that aren’t really about the surface issue — roles, family involvement, communication styles — but about the cultural frames underneath. Naming the frame is often where the work begins.

Our Approach ✶

How we work.

— 01

Neither culture recedes

Active integration of both cultural identities, not subordination of one to the other. Recent research consistently associates this with higher, not lower, relationship satisfaction.

— 02

Building the third culture

The most resilient intercultural relationships build something genuinely new — a blended set of values that belongs only to their particular family — rather than negotiating a compromise between two fixed positions.

— 03

Culturally attuned, not neutral

Honoring identity, heritage, and community as essential context for the work — not as an afterthought, and not by pretending culture doesn’t shape what walks into the room.
